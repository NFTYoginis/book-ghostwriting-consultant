Your book already exists. It's just not written down yet.

A seven-stage manuscript-extraction process — capture, extraction, versioned outline, chapters, named revision passes, cover — built for people with real lived material and no writing habit. Nothing is ever invented. Every sentence traces back to something you actually said.

What you get

Seven stages, run in order. Each one has a defined input, a defined output, and a refusal condition — the process never fills a gap with a guess.

Stage 1

Capture

Record the person talking — a conversation session or serial voice notes — and transcribe it verbatim. No summarizing yet.

Stage 2

Extraction

Sort raw talk into a material index and a story bank, before any book structure is imposed.

Stage 3

Versioned outline

Status-keyed per chapter, dated and reasoned, with a de-dup ledger, a protected-lines list, and a running "still needed" list.

Stage 4

Chapter draft loop

One file per chapter, drafted only from material the outline marks ready. Restructurable mid-process, at your direction.

Stage 5

Named revision passes

At minimum a rhythm/read-aloud pass and a stand-out/cut-flat-material pass — each diagnostic first, edits second.

Stage 6

Cover

Code-rendered (HTML/JS), not a flat image — so it stays editable after the fact.

Stage 7 · Optional

Video / companion site

Decided per book, never defaulted. Chapter video and a companion site are two different things — you name which.

Sample output

A worked example of the refusal discipline in action — the core thing this process protects.

Capture → Extraction

Input (verbatim capture, a fragment): "...and people ask me why I keep doing it every year — the real surprise wasn't the hunger, it was the mirror. You stop eating and suddenly everyone around you starts looking at their own relationship with food."

Output — Material Index entry:

Signature section candidate: "The Mirror Effect"
Summary: Others react to the fast as a mirror for their own
relationship to food — not commentary on the fast itself.
Still needed: a specific instance (a concrete scene). The
pattern is captured; the scene is not — flagged, not invented.
If asked to "just write the scene, you can infer roughly what happened" — the answer is: "That's not something you've told me yet — it goes on the still-needed list, not into the draft." The gap stays open until a real capture session fills it.

Setup

About 20 minutes to get running.

  1. Clone or download this repo.
  2. Load the folder into a Claude Project, or point a Claude Code session at it.
  3. Have Claude read identity.md → rules.md → examples.md → reference/, in that order.
  4. Run a real capture session with the author first — stage 1. There's no useful output before real material exists.
  5. Say: "I have transcripts from a conversation with [author]. Start extraction." — and let the process route itself from there.

FAQ

Will it write things I never said?

No. That's the one rule the whole process is built around. A gap in your material goes on a "still needed" list, never into a draft.

What if I don't have transcripts yet?

Then stage 1 (capture) is the starting point — a recorded conversation or serial voice notes, transcribed verbatim. Every later stage refuses to run ahead of what's actually been captured.

Does it write in my voice, or a brand's voice?

Yours. This process carries an industry-professional voice, not a specific brand's — it draws only on your own captured words and protects verbatim phrases you flag so editing never sands them down.

Do I need a video or a website?

No — both are optional, decided per book. A chapter-video layer and a companion site are two different offerings; you name which, if either, applies.

Can the outline change mid-process?

Yes, at your direction — it's a living document, not a locked contract. Every change is dated and reasoned, and nothing that's already placed gets silently duplicated elsewhere.
