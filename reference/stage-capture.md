# Stage 1 — Capture

## What it is

Get the person talking, and transcribe it verbatim. Nothing else. No summarizing, no organizing, no editing for flow — that work belongs to stage 2 (extraction) and later.

## Real precedent

`GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/source/`:
- `Session-1-Conversation-Notes.pdf` — a structured conversation session.
- `transcripts/IMG_5596.txt`, `IMG_5597.txt`, `IMG_5604.txt` — raw first-person spoken transcripts, transcribed verbatim.

Two capture modes are both valid, drawn from the real trail: a single longer conversation session, or serial voice notes recorded over time. Either produces the same downstream artifact — a verbatim transcript file.

## Mechanics

1. Record the person talking — a conversation (interviewer present) or a solo voice note.
2. Transcribe verbatim. Preserve filler words, false starts, and repetition where they carry meaning (repetition is often a signal of what matters most — see `Gabe-Material-Index.md`'s "SIGNATURE SECTIONS" being the parts the author returned to unprompted).
3. File the transcript with a clear source label (date, session number, or file name) — every later stage needs to trace material back to its transcript.
4. Do not draft, summarize, or outline anything at this stage. Do not skip to extraction mid-session.

## Refusal condition

None — this stage always runs and never refuses input. Everything the person says is valid capture material. The refusal gate begins at stage 2 (only *captured* material can be extracted) and stays active through every later stage.

## Scope guardrail (from the real trail)

A single capture session often contains material for more than one book or more than one thread. The real transcripts contained three overlapping subjects (detox, IIN's primary/secondary foods framework, Ayurveda) and the author himself drew the line on what belonged in the book and what was context only (`Gabe-Material-Index.md`: *"I'll never mention them here… this book is about detox"*). Capture everything; let the author (not this specialist) decide the scope line during extraction.
