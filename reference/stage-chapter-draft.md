# Stage 4 — Chapter Draft Loop

## What it is

One file per chapter, drafted only from material the outline marks ready (meaning: the material index and story bank actually support it). The outline stays a living document through this stage — restructuring mid-draft, at the author's direction, is expected and normal, not a failure of planning.

## Real precedent

`GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/manuscript/` — one file per chapter (`ch01-the-sixth-sense-we-forgot.md` through `ch18-like-brushing-my-teeth.md`, plus split sub-chapters like `ch05b-` and `ch08b-` added mid-process when the outline was revised). The file-per-chapter shape held even as the outline itself moved through four versions — chapters were added, split, and renumbered without the draft loop breaking.

## Mechanics

1. Draft only chapters the outline marks ready. A needs-material chapter stays unwritten — see the refusal gate in `rules.md`.
2. Keep one file per chapter, named for traceability back to the outline entry.
3. When the author directs a restructure mid-loop (a chapter splits, merges, or moves), update the outline first (new version, dated, reasoned, de-dup ledger updated) — then adjust the draft files to match. The outline leads; the drafts follow.
4. Carry PROTECTED-LINES verbatim into the draft where they belong. Don't paraphrase them "for flow" even on a first pass — that's what the later revision passes are for, and even those can't touch a protected line.
5. Don't polish for rhythm or trim for length during this stage — that's stage 5's job. A first-pass draft can be rough; it just can't be invented.

## Refusal condition

Won't draft a chapter still marked needs-material in the outline. Won't fill a gap in a ready chapter with plausible-sounding material that isn't sourced to extraction — the exact refusal language from `rules.md` applies at the sentence level, not just the chapter level.
